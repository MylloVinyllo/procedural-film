# Human Life Cycle native research capture

Captured: 2026-09-26

This file is the research input for the native Procedural Film experiment. It distinguishes sourced life-course facts from invented visual-design rules.

## Source 1 — WHO: life course

Source: https://www.who.int/teams/maternal-newborn-child-adolescent-health-and-ageing/life-course

Relevant facts used downstream:

- WHO frames the life course as a good start to life, optimal development through infancy, childhood, adolescence and youth, adult health and well-being, healthy ageing, and a dignified death.
- Health trajectories are shaped over time by genetic, biological, psychosocial and environmental factors.
- The approach explicitly spans generations.

Drawable implications:

- The film can legitimately move through biological beginning, childhood/adolescence, adulthood and healthy ageing as one trajectory.
- Environmental and social layers can recur throughout the film rather than appearing only in an isolated "social" chapter.
- Intergenerational handoff is an appropriate structural ending, but it must be shown as continuity across generations, not literal repetition of the same person.

## Source 2 — NICHD: pregnancy / zygote

Source: https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/factsheets/pregnancy

Relevant facts used downstream:

- Conception produces a fertilized egg, or zygote.
- The zygote is a cluster of cells that later contributes to fetus and placenta.

Drawable implications:

- Opening biological development may use a discrete 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 cell progression as a symbolic growth motif.
- Exact developmental timing is not encoded in the animation.

## Source 3 — NIMH: The Teen Brain: 7 Things to Know

Source: https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/the-teen-brain-7-things-to-know

Relevant facts used downstream:

- Adolescence is an important period of brain development and maturation.
- Social experiences and peer relationships become especially salient during adolescence.
- The teen brain is adaptive and responsive to experience.

Drawable implications:

- The adolescence passage may shift from inward learning geometry toward a richer peer field.
- The network imagery remains symbolic; it is not presented as a literal connectome.
- We avoid the stereotype that adolescence is inherently irrational or rebellious.

## Source 4 — WHO: social connection / older people

Sources:
- https://www.who.int/teams/social-determinants-of-health/demographic-change-and-healthy-ageing/social-isolation-and-loneliness
- https://www.who.int/health-topics/ageing/reducing-social-isolation-and-loneliness-among-older-people

Relevant facts used downstream:

- High-quality social connection matters across the life course, including older age.
- Social isolation and loneliness can affect people of all ages.

Drawable implications:

- Older age is not visually equated with isolation.
- The protagonist remains embedded in at least one visible relationship late in the film.

## Design-only rules, not sourced scientific claims

The following are art-direction conventions invented for this film:

- The recurring protagonist wears a rust-orange upper garment and dark teal lower garment from later childhood onward so identity survives scale changes and crowds.
- The protagonist has a slightly asymmetrical hair silhouette and a small warm-gold chest/path marker.
- Other people use cool blue, sage and muted rose garments, never one identical crowd colour.
- Hands, faces and clothing folds are simplified ink illustration, not medical anatomy.
- The home, school-like table, street/meadow, workshop/table and older-age garden are narrative environments chosen for legibility, not claims that these experiences are universal.
- A warm trajectory line, a gold transfer point and the recurring circular geometry are symbolic continuity devices.
