# Library of Congress — disc cleaning workflow

Checked: 2026-09-27

Source:
https://www.loc.gov/collections/national-jukebox/articles-and-essays/making-the-jukebox/

## Source-derived facts

For discs prepared for preservation digitisation, the Library of Congress describes a concrete workflow:
- treat the disc on a record-cleaner turntable with a mild cleaning solution;
- gently scrub with a soft brush;
- vacuum the liquid away;
- rinse with distilled water;
- clean/rinse both sides before re-sleeving.

The Library also states elsewhere that its sound-preservation operation uses vacuuming techniques to remove dust and microscopic particles from record grooves.

## Story implications

V3 can depict a truthful cleaning sequence without magical “instant purification”:
1. record is secured on a cleaning platter;
2. fluid is applied;
3. soft brush makes controlled contact while the disc rotates;
4. vacuum arm/contact removes liquid;
5. surface is shown dry / ready;
6. record returns to sleeve or turntable.

The hero action should be the hand/tool relationship, not a generic glowing effect.

## Do not infer

The exact cleaning chemistry, dwell time, number of rotations and machine geometry vary by method. Those details need either a product-specific manual or clearly labelled drawing conventions.
