## Relevant facts captured

- Rainfall can infiltrate soil or become surface runoff; proportions depend on rainfall, soil, saturation, vegetation, slope and impervious surfaces.
- Sandy soils generally admit water faster than clay-rich soils.
- Vegetation can slow runoff and give water more time to infiltrate.
- Impervious surfaces such as roads and parking lots accelerate runoff toward drains and streams.
- In the unsaturated zone, pores contain both air and water; below the water table, pores are saturated.
- Groundwater moves through pores and fractures and can discharge into streams and oceans.
- Streamflow combines runoff, groundwater/baseflow and other inputs, then carries water downhill toward larger rivers and oceans.

## Drawing implications

A soil macro shot can contrast air-filled and water-filled pore space. A city surface can legitimately accelerate the tracked water toward a storm drain. Stream/river scenes should show tributary merging and flow direction rather than a static blue band.
