## Relevant facts captured

- Solar energy drives evaporation from oceans and other water bodies.
- Evaporation from oceans, seas, lakes and rivers supplies most atmospheric moisture.
- Plants move water from roots through tissues and release water vapor through stomata in leaves.
- Rising air carries vapor upward; cooler air favours condensation into cloud droplets.
- Evaporation and condensation are opposite phase changes and can be linked visually as surface molecules leaving liquid, then re-forming droplets aloft.

## Drawing implications

The final ocean-to-cloud sequence can begin with a sun-heated surface, separate individual vapor trajectories from the liquid surface, then show cooling/condensation around aerosol nuclei. If a plant-transpiration branch is shown, vapor should leave the leaf through stomatal regions rather than from an arbitrary glowing point.
