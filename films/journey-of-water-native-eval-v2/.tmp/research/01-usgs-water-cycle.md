# USGS Water Cycle

Checked: 2026-09-26
Source: U.S. Geological Survey, Water Science School
URL: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/water-cycle

## Relevant facts captured

- The water cycle describes where water is stored and how it moves between atmosphere, land surface, oceans, rivers, lakes, soil and groundwater.
- Water can move as liquid, solid or gas and can change phase as it moves.
- Atmosphere/surface exchange includes evaporation, evapotranspiration and precipitation.
- Surface movement includes runoff and streamflow.
- Subsurface movement includes infiltration, groundwater recharge and groundwater flow.
- Groundwater can discharge back to rivers, ocean and springs.
- Oceans hold roughly 96% of Earth’s water, making the ocean the dominant long-term visual reservoir.
- Human use changes where water is stored, how it moves and how clean it is.

## Drawing implications

The film can truthfully move from ocean → vapor/cloud → precipitation → land → runoff/infiltration → streams/rivers → human use → sea → evaporation. The cycle has no privileged beginning; the opening/ending loop is therefore conceptually valid.

## Do not infer

Do not imply that all precipitation follows one route. At landfall, water can split into runoff, infiltration, plant uptake and later evaporation/transpiration.
