# USGS Condensation and Precipitation

Checked: 2026-09-26
Sources:

- https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle

## Relevant facts captured

- Condensation changes water vapor into liquid water and is crucial to cloud formation.
- Cloud droplets form around tiny particles such as dust, salt and smoke.
- Cloud droplets can be around 10 micrometres and may grow by condensation and coalescence.
- Most condensed cloud water does not immediately fall; droplets must grow enough that their fall speed exceeds supporting updrafts.
- Millions of cloud droplets may be required to form a single precipitation-sized raindrop.
- Precipitation is the main route by which atmospheric water returns to Earth’s surface.

## Drawing implications

Cloud scenes should show many suspended microdroplets/nuclei feeding a smaller number of larger droplets. A falling raindrop should emerge from a collective cloud process rather than appearing from a single isolated vapor particle.
