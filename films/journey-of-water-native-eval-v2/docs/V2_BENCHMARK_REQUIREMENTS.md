# The Journey of Water — V2 benchmark requirements

Purpose: make the second Procedural Film production case a genuine attempt to reach Butterfly-level density, continuity and temporal storytelling rather than merely complete the pipeline.

## Control

Primary control: `examples/butterfly-life`.

This document does not replace the native Procedural Film workflow. It sharpens what “on brief” means for this benchmark.

## What the first full case taught us

The Human Life Cycle V2 proved that the end-to-end workflow works, but it still lagged Butterfly Life in:
- complexity of motion inside each shot;
- amount of subject-specific detail;
- recognisability of the main subject;
- exposition carried by the picture itself;
- artistic attractiveness;
- continuity from shot to shot;
- density of meaningful secondary elements;
- transition design;
- sense of one whole work rather than adjacent cards.

The Water film is designed specifically to stress those dimensions.

## Benchmark target

The film should be understandable without explanatory text and should feel like one continuous visual journey.

A representative illustrated shot should normally contain:
- a clearly readable hero state;
- at least three depth layers;
- a real environment rather than a neutral field;
- multiple secondary objects that react to or contextualise the hero;
- surface detail/hatching specific to the material;
- one primary action plus secondary motion;
- camera/parallax or an equivalent depth cue;
- at least one visual bridge to the next shot.

A representative schematic shot should normally contain:
- the physical system being explained, not a decorative symbol;
- internal structure;
- at least two simultaneous processes;
- measurement/guide geometry;
- one highlighted causal change;
- a clear match-cut relation to the illustrated plate.

## Motion target

Every shot needs a temporal arc, not just an entrance animation.

Check for:
- mass and inertia;
- acceleration/deceleration consistent with the subject;
- secondary motion;
- overlapping action;
- environmental response;
- changing camera relation;
- state change over the full shot;
- at least three materially different states across the 6-sample contact sheet.

Water-specific motion must distinguish:
- falling drop;
- impact/splash;
- adhesion and sliding on a leaf;
- infiltration through pores/soil;
- stream turbulence;
- river transport;
- pipe/channel flow;
- wave/surface motion;
- evaporation/plume ascent;
- cloud condensation.

## Transition target

Transitions are designed before scene implementation.

Use a deliberate mixture of:
- shape match;
- scale match;
- directional continuation;
- camera pass-through;
- occlusion;
- surface-to-system macro push;
- line/path continuation;
- particle-to-particle morph;
- hard cut on conserved geometry.

No transition should exist only because the timeline advances.

## Density target

Do not optimise for source-line count. Use the rendered image as the test.

At quarter-scale, each shot must still read immediately.
At full frame, the viewer should discover secondary detail after the main action is understood.

Butterfly Life remains the control for:
- subject-specific construction;
- microtexture;
- bespoke scene geometry;
- integrated annotations;
- background activity.

## Continuity target

The hero is not a cartoon droplet with a face. Continuity comes from recurring physical geometry:
- the tracked water parcel / highlight contour;
- a recurring cycle-gold tracer point;
- the same curvature or internal glint carried through match cuts;
- consistent flow direction where physically appropriate.

When the tracked parcel joins a larger body of water, the film follows a highlighted subset/streamline without falsely implying that one literal molecule remains isolated.

## Critic rule

A green technical gate is never accepted as visual completion.

For each scene:
1. six-frame contact sheet;
2. inspect every frame;
3. compare against the storyboard and this benchmark;
4. compare representative density against Butterfly;
5. fix all P1/P2 visual issues;
6. re-snap after material fixes.

Whole-film critic waves must explicitly judge:
- readability;
- motion richness;
- exposition;
- transition quality;
- visual continuity;
- scene density;
- artistic attractiveness;
- coherence of the complete work.
