# Art bible · Human Life Cycle

The visual rules every scene follows.
This film keeps the established Procedural Film house style. No separate reference-analysis pass is needed.

## 1. Frame

1080×1920, 24 fps.

Safe area for must-read meaning:
- x 60–940
- y 220–1540

Compose for the tall frame. Human figures should use verticality rather than being cropped from a square composition.

## 2. Palette

### 2.1 House palette

Keep the warm paper / dark ink / muted natural colours for illustrated plates and deep navy / pale lavender / white / magenta accent for blueprint plates.

### 2.2 Human-life extension

Named semantic colours:
- `selfWarm` = #C86F4B — the individual trajectory / warm human accent
- `socialBlue` = #6FA6B8 — relationships / social field
- `memoryViolet` = #80729C — memory / accumulated experience
- `cycleGold` = #D9A23E — recurrence / transfer / return
- `ageSage` = #8C9B7C — later-life calm / persistence

No colour means “good” or “bad”. Colour is structural, not moral.

## 3. Line and shape

Illustrated figures:
- dark irregular ink contour;
- interior hatching used for depth, not gradient shading;
- silhouettes must read on a phone at contact-sheet scale.

Blueprint figures / systems:
- double-line anatomy or system outlines;
- pale grid / guide circles;
- nodes and branches;
- magenta reserved for transformation events.

## 4. Tone and grain

Use the existing house paper grain, hatch, stipple and 12 fps boil.
Character/object motion uses on-twos.
Camera and overlay motion remain 24 fps.

## 5. Schematic language

Blueprint scenes explain internal or systemic transitions:
- cell division;
- neural / learning network;
- social network as system;
- memory / accumulated relations;
- final return to a seed/cell motif.

No explanatory paragraphs inside schematic shots.

## 6. Illustrated overlays

Overlays show:
- individual path;
- attention;
- relationship;
- time;
- scale.

A recurring thin `cycleGold` ring is the progress glyph across the film.
A `selfWarm` trajectory follows the primary individual.
`socialBlue` branches appear only when relationships are semantically present.

## 7. Motion language

Primary semantic actions:
- divide;
- grow;
- stand / step;
- learn / connect;
- enter a group;
- diverge from group rhythm;
- reconnect;
- create / contribute;
- slow / age;
- transfer;
- return.

Forbidden as the primary event:
- pan/zoom over an otherwise static card;
- generic crossfade standing in for growth;
- decorative particles replacing a relationship;
- a crowd that erases the primary individual;
- text carrying the story that motion failed to show.

## 8. Match cuts

Recurring shapes:
- G1: seed/cell circle at frame centre;
- G2: head/face ellipse aligned to the seed circle;
- G3: social-network node aligned to the face;
- G4: closing child/new-cell circle aligned back to G1.

The exact pixel geometry is specified in the storyboard.

## 9. Wordmark

Working wordmark: `human`.
Use only in the final blueprint shot, following house wordmark rules.

## 10. Subject reference

Sources: NICHD Pregnancy; NIMH The Teen Brain; WHO Life Course; WHO Social Connection.

### 10.1 Zygote / early division

Draw a single circular cell becoming a small cluster through discrete division events.
Do not imply exact timing beyond the broad sequence.

### 10.2 Infant / child

Readability comes from proportion:
- relatively large head;
- shorter limbs;
- unstable first-step posture;
- later child scenes use more energetic, open limb poses.

### 10.3 Adolescent

Transition toward adult proportions.
The key story property is not “rebellion”, but increasing social salience plus continuing development.

### 10.4 Adult

Adult figure is the visual anchor for the individual/social comparison.
Keep one identifiable individual silhouette while surrounding figures multiply and connect.

### 10.5 Older adult

Ageing is shown through posture, tempo, accumulated line texture and changed gait, not caricature.
The older figure remains socially connected in at least one shot.

### 10.6 Social network

A network is a visual abstraction of relationships, not a literal neurological structure.
Use variable clusters and connection strengths.
The primary individual's node remains visually identifiable.

### 10.7 Mistakes to avoid

- Wrong: “individual” means isolated from all people.
  Correct: individual identity remains visible inside social relations.

- Wrong: “social human” means a faceless crowd.
  Correct: relationships are shown as interactions, roles and connection patterns.

- Wrong: ageing equals collapse.
  Correct: later life changes pace and posture while continuity and connection remain.

- Wrong: final newborn/cell means the same person literally repeats.
  Correct: the loop is symbolic and intergenerational.

- Wrong: brain/network imagery claims deterministic behaviour.
  Correct: blueprint shots communicate development and relationship structure only.
