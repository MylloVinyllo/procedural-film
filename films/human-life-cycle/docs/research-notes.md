# Research notes · Human Life Cycle

Checked 2026-09-25.

The film uses four authoritative sources as factual anchors. The film's philosophy and symbolism are artistic interpretation, not claims made by these sources.

## R1 · NICHD · Pregnancy / conception

Source:
https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/factsheets/pregnancy

Useful facts for drawing:
- pregnancy begins from conception, when sperm penetrates an egg;
- the fertilized egg is a zygote;
- the zygote travels toward the uterus and forms a cluster of cells;
- those cells later contribute to the fetus and placenta;
- pregnancy is commonly described across trimesters.

Permitted visual translation:
- one fertilized cell;
- deterministic division / multiplication;
- transition from a single cell motif into an early human-development motif.

Do not infer:
- exact microscopic timing not stated in the storyboard;
- detailed embryology beyond what the shot needs.

## R2 · NIMH · The Teen Brain

Source:
https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/the-teen-brain-7-things-to-know

Useful facts for drawing:
- adolescence remains an important period of brain development;
- maturation continues into the mid-to-late twenties;
- social experiences and peer relationships become especially salient during adolescence;
- the brain remains adaptive and responsive to experience.

Permitted visual translation:
- network refinement;
- stronger / weaker connections;
- individual figure moving into a field of peers;
- learning and social attention as a bridge from solitary development into social identity.

Do not infer:
- one fixed universal adolescent personality;
- deterministic behaviour from a brain diagram.

## R3 · WHO · Life-course approach

Source:
https://www.who.int/teams/maternal-newborn-child-adolescent-health-and-ageing/life-course

Useful facts for drawing:
- a life-course approach spans a good start to life, development through childhood/adolescence/youth, adult health and well-being, healthy ageing, and dignified death;
- trajectories are shaped over time by genetic, biological, psychosocial and environmental factors;
- experiences in critical and sensitive periods can affect later outcomes;
- the perspective includes intergenerational health.

Permitted visual translation:
- one continuous life trajectory;
- earlier forms leaving traces in later forms;
- intergenerational hand-off / recurrence.

Do not infer:
- that every individual's life follows an identical social path.

## R4 · WHO · Social Connection

Sources:
https://www.who.int/teams/social-determinants-of-health/demographic-change-and-healthy-ageing/social-isolation-and-loneliness
https://www.who.int/health-topics/ageing/reducing-social-isolation-and-loneliness-among-older-people

Useful facts for drawing:
- high-quality social connection is important to mental and physical health and well-being;
- social isolation and loneliness affect people across ages;
- WHO treats social connection as a life-course issue.

Permitted visual translation:
- social connection as a real human condition rather than decorative crowd imagery;
- networks of relationships that can strengthen, thin or persist over time;
- older age still embedded in social connection rather than depicted only as solitary decline.

Do not infer:
- that social connectedness removes individuality;
- that a large network is inherently better than a small one.

## Story phases supported by the research

1. biological beginning;
2. early development;
3. childhood growth / learning;
4. adolescence and stronger social salience;
5. adulthood as both individual and relational;
6. ageing across the life course;
7. intergenerational continuation.

## Artistic interpretation layered on top

These are film concepts, not scientific claims:
- a recurring ring represents continuity / cycle;
- a single warm line represents individual trajectory;
- a branching network represents social connection and accumulated relationships;
- match cuts between cell / eye / crowd node / new cell create the sense of recurrence;
- the ending loops visually into the opening.
