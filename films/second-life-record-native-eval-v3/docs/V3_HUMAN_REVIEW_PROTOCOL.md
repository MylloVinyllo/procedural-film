# V3 Human Review Protocol

Purpose: test whether V3 communicates its story without prior explanation.

This is deliberately short and observational. It is not a scoring exercise.

## Pass 1 — blind watch

Watch the 30 s master once from beginning to end.

Before rereading the storyboard or this document, write one sentence answering:

> What happened in the film?

Do not try to use the production vocabulary. Describe only what you actually understood.

## Pass 2 — story beats

Watch again and note, in your own words, whether you recognised these events:

- a person finds/selects a record;
- the record appears dirty/problematic;
- the record is played;
- the playback sounds bad/noisy;
- the person decides to clean it;
- the record is visibly cleaned;
- liquid is removed / the record is dried;
- the same record returns to the turntable;
- it is played again;
- the second result feels better and the listener enjoys it.

For any missing beat, note what you thought the image meant instead.

## Pass 3 — ambiguity map

Record the exact moments where one of these happened:

- you could not tell what an object was;
- you could not tell what the hand was doing;
- you did not understand why the scene changed;
- the comic effect looked like a physical object;
- a physical object looked like an abstract symbol;
- the first and second playback did not feel meaningfully different;
- a transition was visually confusing;
- motion was too weak to carry the intended action;
- there was too little detail to make the scene believable.

The most useful note format is:

`time / scene → what I saw → what I think it was supposed to mean`

Example:

`00:16.5 → orange bar moved across black circle → I think this was the brush cleaning the record`

## Pass 4 — craft comparison

Only after the comprehension passes, compare V3 to the Butterfly benchmark in these concrete dimensions:

- subject specificity of the drawing;
- character acting / pose clarity;
- richness of secondary elements;
- number of simultaneous meaningful motions;
- transformation / transition sophistication;
- environmental depth;
- camera grammar;
- visual attractiveness as a finished work.

Do not collapse these into one generic “better/worse” reaction. The goal is to locate the next production bottleneck.

## Review output

The review can be as informal as a voice/text reaction.

For durable continuation, the useful minimum is:

1. one-sentence story you understood;
2. first moment you became confused, if any;
3. which scene felt weakest visually;
4. whether first vs second playback difference was obvious;
5. whether the cleaning action itself was understandable;
6. one or two specific things Butterfly still does much better.

Those observations become the input to the next correction or V4 design transaction.
