# Pro-Ject / Technics — vacuum cleaning and playback mechanics

Checked: 2026-09-27

Sources:
- Pro-Ject VC-S3 user guide: https://www.project-audio.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/VC-S3-Manual.pdf
- Technics SL-1500C operating instructions: https://www.technics.com/content/dam/Panasonic/nz/pdf-download/Operating-Instructions-SL-1500C.pdf

## Source-derived facts — Pro-Ject

The VC-S3 manual gives a recognisable wet/vacuum cleaning sequence:
- distribute cleaning fluid across the record;
- allow time for impurities to dissolve if needed;
- rotate the record;
- place the vacuum arm over the record;
- lower/engage the vacuum arm while vacuum is running;
- vacuum off the fluid;
- allow the record to dry before removal;
- repeat for the opposite side.

The manual recommends limited vacuum rotations rather than prolonged vacuuming.

## Source-derived facts — Technics

The Technics instructions give a recognisable playback sequence:
- place the record on the platter;
- remove stylus protection / free the tonearm as appropriate;
- start turntable rotation;
- move/lower the tonearm so the stylus contacts the record.

## Story implications

For V3, the first and second playback can mirror the same physical sequence:
record on platter → platter rotates → tonearm pivots → stylus descends → listener reaction.

The cleaning scene can visibly differ from playback because the disc sits on a cleaning platter with a brush/fluid/vacuum-contact sequence.

## Do not infer

The V3 machine is generic/unbranded unless a later brief explicitly switches to a Myllo Vinyllo product model. Exact control locations, vacuum-arm shape and proportions are therefore production design choices, not claims about a specific commercial device.
